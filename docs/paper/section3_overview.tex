\section{System Overview}
\label{sec:overview}

BGP Tracer is a two-phase analysis system that turns noisy routing and
traffic alerts into evidence-backed incident verdicts. Its design
priorities are interpretability, reproducibility, and graceful
degradation. Rather than treating detection as a single classification
problem, the system separates telemetry acquisition, anomaly
detection, correlation triage, causal attribution, and operator
reporting. The components exchange structured evidence through a
shared cache, so that every decision can be traced to the observations
and detector outputs that produced it. Ground-truth labels are excluded
from the analysis path.

Figure~\ref{fig:system-overview} illustrates the overall workflow. An
analysis starts with a candidate event specified by an AS, a set of
prefixes, and a time window. The system combines control-plane
telemetry from routing monitors and data-plane telemetry from traffic
aggregates. The routing detectors identify hijack, route-leak, and
outage-related signals, while the traffic detector compares the
observed series with a periodic baseline or a non-periodic fallback
when the historical data do not support a stable period. The detectors
emit structured evidence rather than final verdicts, which allows the
same observations to be reused by later stages.

\emph{Phase~1} performs deterministic correlation triage. It aligns
the routing and traffic observations on a common time grid, preserves
their timing offsets, and evaluates whether the two signals are both
present and temporally related. The resulting verdict is one of five
core classes: \texttt{real\_incident}, \texttt{silent\_alert},
\texttt{traffic\_only}, \texttt{correlation\_uncertain}, or
\texttt{no\_clear\_anomaly}. Phase~1 does not infer a causal direction;
it determines whether an event warrants deeper analysis. Missing
telemetry is reported explicitly and is not interpreted as an absence
of anomalies.

A shared evidence cache connects the two phases. It stores detector
outputs, aligned timestamps, statistical summaries, missing-data
conditions, and decision provenance. Caching separates the
time-sensitive triage path from deeper attribution and reporting, and
allows an operator to audit how a particular verdict was reached. It
also preserves the distinction between a complete verdict and a
partial verdict produced when a detector or data source is
unavailable.

\emph{Phase~2} performs deeper evidence attribution on the cached
observations. It uses a breakpoint-anchored pre/post comparison as a
PNS-inspired intervention proxy, supported by bootstrap confidence
intervals and permutation tests. Additional routing-path, cascade
scope, Granger, and transfer-entropy evidence is synthesized to assess
the causal direction, incident type, and impact scope. The phase
records whether a directional estimate satisfies the causal-support
criteria and abstains when the evidence is insufficient or
conflicting. It does not overwrite the Phase~1 correlation verdict;
the two outputs describe different levels of evidence.

The final output contains the correlation verdict, directional
evidence, cause and impact estimates, and the associated evidence
chain. The review queue is ordered by evidence-based impact rather than
raw alert frequency, so that incidents affecting substantial traffic or
multiple downstream networks are surfaced first. Low-impact, uncertain,
and insufficiently supported events remain available with explicit
status labels instead of being silently discarded.
