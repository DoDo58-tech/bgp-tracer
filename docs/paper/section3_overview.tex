\section{System Overview}
\label{sec:overview}

BGP Tracer is a two-phase analysis system that turns noisy routing and
traffic alerts into evidence-backed incident verdicts. Its design
priorities are interpretability, reproducibility, and graceful
degradation. Rather than treating detection as a single classification
problem, the system separates telemetry acquisition, anomaly detection,
correlation triage, causal attribution, and operator reporting. The
components exchange structured evidence through a shared cache, so that
every decision can be traced to the observations and detector outputs
that produced it. Ground-truth labels are excluded from the analysis
path.

An analysis starts with a candidate event specified by an AS, a set of
prefixes, and a time window. The routing detectors identify hijack,
route-leak, and outage-related signals, while the traffic detector
compares the observed series with a periodic baseline or a non-periodic
fallback when the historical data do not support a stable period. The
detectors emit structured evidence rather than final verdicts, which
allows the same observations to be reused by later stages.

\emph{Phase~1} performs deterministic correlation triage. It aligns the
routing and traffic observations on a common time grid, preserves their
timing offsets, and evaluates whether the two signals are both present
and temporally related. The resulting verdict is one of five core
classes: \texttt{real\_incident}, \texttt{silent\_alert},
\texttt{traffic\_only}, \texttt{correlation\_uncertain}, or
\texttt{no\_clear\_anomaly}. Phase~1 does not infer a causal direction;
it determines whether an event warrants deeper analysis. Missing
telemetry is reported explicitly and is not interpreted as an absence of
anomalies.

A shared evidence cache connects the two phases. It stores detector
outputs, aligned timestamps, statistical summaries, missing-data
conditions, and decision provenance, which separates the time-sensitive
triage path from deeper attribution and reporting and lets an operator
audit how a verdict was reached. It also preserves the distinction
between a complete verdict and a partial verdict produced when a
detector or data source is unavailable.

\emph{Phase~2} performs deeper evidence attribution on the cached
observations. It segments each series on the shared grid, reports each
onset as an interval of placements the data cannot distinguish, and
orders the two edges only when those intervals are disjoint. A direction
is committed only if four vetoes pass---precedence, persistence,
exclusivity against placebo onsets, and provenance plus mirror checks
(\S\ref{sec:method-vetoes})---and a refusal returns the veto that failed
with the statistic behind it. It does not overwrite the Phase-1
correlation verdict; the two outputs describe different levels of
evidence.

The final output contains the correlation verdict, directional evidence,
cause and impact estimates, and the associated evidence chain. The review
queue is ordered by evidence-based impact rather than raw alert
frequency, so that incidents affecting substantial traffic or multiple
downstream networks are surfaced first. Low-impact, uncertain, and
insufficiently supported events remain available with explicit status
labels instead of being silently discarded.
