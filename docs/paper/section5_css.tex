The full derivation of the Pre/post-intervention
Difference-in-Means (\pdm, originally CSS) --- including
the bootstrap-CI and permutation-$p$-value machinery ---
is in the supplementary material. We give here the
equations and the decision rule that are necessary to
understand the Phase~2 architecture.

\paragraph{Pearl's PNS as motivation.}
Pearl's \pns{} \cite{pearl1999, tian-pearl-2000, maley2018, duarte2024} is the
\emph{motivating} framework for the directional score.
Pearl's PNS is defined for binary $X, Y$ as:
$\mathrm{PNS} = P(Y{=}1 \mid X{=}1) - P(Y{=}1 \mid X{=}0)$.
Our BGP telemetry is a sparse 0/1 series and we do
\emph{not} have a causal graph to identify
counterfactuals; the implementation therefore
computes a non-parametric difference-in-means on
the pre/post windows, which is the
\emph{continuous-analogue} form:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{PDM}(R\!\to\!T) \;=\; \bar{T}_{\mathrm{post}} - \bar{T}_{\mathrm{pre}} \;\;\approx\;\; \mathbb{E}[T \mid \mathrm{do}(R{=}1)] - \mathbb{E}[T \mid \mathrm{do}(R{=}0)].
\label{eq:css-def}
\end{equation}
We stress that the \emph{estimator} is a
difference-in-means; Pearl's PNS is the
\emph{interpretation} (i.e.\ "is the post-window
sufficient to explain the observed $T$ effect?").
A reviewer who reads the implementation as
do-calculus will be misled; we therefore use the
PDM abbreviation in the main text (footnote
\ref{fn:pdm-css}) and reserve the Pearl-PNS
language for the motivation paragraph only.

\paragraph{Point estimator and bootstrap CI.}
The point estimator is the difference-in-means
$\widehat{\mathrm{PDM}}(R\!\to\!T) = \bar{T}_{\mathrm{post}}
- \bar{T}_{\mathrm{pre}}$ with the pre/post split at the
MSBD breakpoint. The bootstrap 95\% CI is the empirical
2.5\%/97.5\% quantile of $B\in[500,2000]$ non-parametric
bootstrap replicates. \figref{fig:css-illustration} shows
the PDM computation on a representative timeline.

\paragraph{Permutation $p$-value.}
We use a permutation test on the pooled
$U := [T_{\mathrm{pre}}; T_{\mathrm{post}}]$:
\begin{equation}
p \;=\; \Pr_{b}\!\left[\, \big|\overline{U^{(b)}_{\mathrm{post}}} - \overline{U^{(b)}_{\mathrm{pre}}}\big| \;\geq\; |\widehat{\mathrm{PDM}}| \,\right].
\label{eq:css-perm}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Decision rule.}
PDM declares a direction iff all three hold: the
bootstrap 95\% CI excludes zero, the raw
$\widehat{\mathrm{PDM}}$ exceeds the alternative
direction's CI upper bound, and $p < \alpha$ with
default $\alpha=0.05$. Otherwise PDM returns
\texttt{unknown}.

\paragraph{What PDM commits on its strict-causal subset.}
\label{sec:css-strict-subset}
On the v17 ground-truth, the PDM engine returns
\path{is_causal = True} on 3/30 events; on the remaining
27/30 it abstains (\Sectref{sec:css-sparsity}). The 3
strict-causal events are real BGP-monitoring events on
which the bootstrap 95\% CI on
$\widehat{\mathrm{PDM}}(R\!\to\!T)$ excludes zero, the
permutation $p$-value crosses $\alpha = 0.05$, and the
raw estimator exceeds the alternative direction's CI
upper bound. \figref{fig:css-n3} shows the point
estimate and 95\% bootstrap CI for the 3 events.

The 3/30 PDM strict-causal subset is the falsifiable
significance core of the Phase~2 design. The
$\overline{L_1}$ shift between the PDM prior and the
composite scoring-rule output is 0.265 on this subset (the supplementary
\Sectref{sec:supp-css} reports the per-channel
contributions). The three events are dominated by
routing-side signals (CTPR, CIC, MSBD) and the
traffic-side Granger channel contributes 0.05 to the
scoring-rule output on each --- a 1.5$\times$ over the
$1/30$ Traffic-only base rate. This is the evidence
that PDM, when it does fire, produces a non-trivial
causal commitment; the abstention on the other 27
events is what keeps the system from over-firing.

\paragraph{Why PDM abstains on most events.}
\label{sec:css-sparsity}
On the v17 ground-truth ($N{=}30$ events) PDM is
strict-causal ($p<0.05$) on only $\frac{\le 3}{30}$
events ($\leq 10.0\%$). The abstention is structural:
The pre/post-intervention windows are short
(typically $N_{\mathrm{pre}}{=}3$ and
$N_{\mathrm{post}}{=}6$ buckets); the smallest achievable
$p$-value is $1/(B{+}1) \approx 0.005$ for $B=200$
permutations, below which $\alpha=0.05$ is already set.
Loosening $\alpha$ does not manufacture rejection where
the permutation distribution overlaps. When \css{}
abstains, the five evidence channels feed the Bayesian
synthesis (\Sectref{sec:scoring-rule}), which commits a
direction with a calibrated scoring-rule output. We do
\emph{not} claim that \css{} is a stand-alone classifier;
we claim that \css{} is a falsifiable significance layer
whose abstention is what makes the Bayesian synthesis
safe.

\paragraph{Temporal precedence.}
For $\mathrm{CSS}(R\!\to\!T)$ the routing intervention
onset must precede the traffic intervention onset by at
least one bucket; the gate is
$\Delta T \geq 1$ (and $\leq -1$ for the reverse
direction). The \css{} engine is implemented in
\path{agents/css_engine.py} and exposes
\path{css_score(routing_timeline, traffic_timeline, n_bootstrap=1000)}
returning
\path{{css_r2t, css_t2r, direction, confidence, p_value, ci_lower, ci_upper, is_causal: bool}}.
