\begin{abstract}
Internet operators rely on BGP monitoring platforms to detect routing
anomalies, yet these platforms produce thousands of daily alerts while
few are confirmed as actionable. Triage by static thresholds and
heuristic rules cannot separate a traffic disturbance caused by a
routing change from one that merely coincides with it. We present BGP
Tracer, a two-phase attribution system over RIPE RIS routing telemetry
and Cloudflare Radar traffic aggregates. Phase~1 runs detectors on a
shared five-minute grid and returns a categorical verdict that filters
benign events. Phase~2 applies an admissible edge attribution (AEA)
test: each series is segmented, the onset of a change is reported as an
\emph{interval} rather than a point, and a direction is committed only
if four vetoes pass---disjoint onset intervals in the right order,
persisting effect, exclusivity against placebo onsets, and provenance
and mirror checks. Every refusal returns the veto that failed and the
statistic behind it. Two consequences are measured. First, ordering by
onset \emph{intervals} instead of point estimates removes the dominant
failure mode: on a simultaneous common shock, false directional
commitments fall from $9.7\%$ to $0.7\%$ of $600$ windows,
while planted mechanisms are recovered with $100\%$ precision in all
twelve lag--magnitude cells ($2{,}291$ commitments, none wrong).
Second, the test is conservative at independence ($3.0\%$ rejections at
$\alpha=0.05$) and anti-conservative under serial dependence
($12.3\%$ at lag-one correlation $0.8$), which we report rather than
round to nominal. On the public corpus now in the tree the primary
result is a coverage result: $200$ of $227$ cached windows cannot produce
both series, and all $27$ that can stop at the first veto---unchanged
when the exclusivity level is raised to $0.99$, which locates the
abstention in the data rather than in the threshold. Each stop returns
its reason, so the refusals form an auditable record of what was missing
from the evidence rather than a silent decline.



\vspace{1ex}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} BGP routing, anomaly detection, causal inference, root-cause analysis, traffic attribution, network dependability.
\end{abstract}
