\begin{abstract}
\label{sec:abstract}
Internet anomaly attribution is a joint decision:
an operator sees a routing-plane change and a
co-temporal data-plane traffic change, and must
commit to (i)~a root-cause class ---
\textit{origin hijack}, \textit{route leak},
\textit{outage}, or \textit{no clear anomaly} --- and
(ii)~a causal direction between the two signals.
The practical reality of BGP-anomaly monitoring is
that \emph{the bottleneck is not detector
sensitivity but attribution dependability}: on the
30-event v17 ground-truth, raw single-signal
detectors emit 1.18 million alert events in total
--- a median of 5{,}286 alerts per 1.5-hour window
and 21.6\% of them are noise from other detectors
firing on the same window. Operators drown in
unsorted alerts without an arbiter that can
adjudicate which detector's verdict is the right
one. Existing systems address one decision but
rarely both: classifiers
\cite{artemis, rpki-validator, cloudflare-radar}
emit a root-cause label without a falsifiable
significance claim, and correlators
\cite{granger1969, transfer-entropy-internet} emit
a direction without arbitration when root-cause
signals conflict. A dependable system must commit
on both questions while remaining interpretable,
reproducible, and robust to data pathologies of
real Internet telemetry.

We present \textbf{BGP Tracer}, a two-phase
analyzer whose design priorities are
\emph{interpretability} (every verdict is
traceable to a detector output),
\emph{reproducibility} (every number is
regenerable by one shell command), and
\emph{graceful degradation} (every degraded mode
produces a valid output, not a crash).
\textbf{Phase~1} (\emph{Dependable Root-Cause
Arbitration}) runs six single-signal detectors
and reduces them to a five-class verdict via a
deterministic arbitration
(\path{compute_top_cause}) with explicit caps,
gates, and tie-breakers. \textbf{Phase~2}
(\emph{Causal Direction Anchoring}) feeds the
same cache to a composite scoring rule anchored
on a pre/post-intervention difference-in-means
test (\css{}) with bootstrap CI and permutation
$p$-value, and synthesises five evidence channels
into a calibrated 3-way direction commitment.

We evaluate BGP Tracer on a curated 30-event v17
ground-truth (7 origin-hijack, 8 route-leak, 14
BGP-reachability-outage plus one negative
control). The \emph{operator-facing primary
result} is the end-to-end HTML pipeline strict-
accuracy: $\mathbf{26/30 = 86.7\%}$
(Wilson 95\% CI $[70.3\%, 94.7\%]$) post-fix
(with the deterministic \path{skip_outage=False}
configuration), matching the deterministic
Phase~1 86.7\% on the same 30 events. The
\emph{diagnostic numbers} are: deterministic
Phase~1 86.7\% (cache-amortised, no LLM);
end-to-end HTML pipeline 70.0\% pre-fix
(Wilson 95\% CI $[52.1\%, 83.3\%]$) and
86.7\% post-fix (the 16.7~pp gap is closed by a
one-line flag change); unseen-event stress test
on 13 v18 candidates 6/10 = 60.0\%
(Wilson 95\% CI $[31.3\%, 83.2\%]$) on
sufficient-data events, with the 13$\to$10
reduction attributable to graceful-degradation
for \path{light_mode} pollution in the v18
cache build. The \emph{detector-arbitration
contribution} is bounded by a three-tier
negative-control experiment: no-fixes baseline
70.0\%, single-cap half-fixes 79.3\%, full
ten-fix arbitration 86.7\% (each tier 7--9 pp).
A 27-of-30 abstention rate on the \pdm{} strict-
causal test is structural (Pearl's
counterfactual on 5-minute \cf{} Radar buckets
is sparse; \Sectref{sec:css-sparsity}); the
\pdm{} engine commits a falsifiable direction on
3/30 v17 events
(\figref{fig:css-n3}), and the composite scoring
rule commits a direction on the rest. End-to-end
reproduction takes one shell command on the
released artifact; all sources, scripts, and data
are open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have presented \textbf{BGP Tracer}, a two-phase
analyzer that addresses the joint Internet anomaly
attribution problem with a design whose priorities
are interpretability, reproducibility, and graceful
degradation. The motivation is operational: on the
30-event v17 ground-truth, raw single-signal
detectors emit 1.18 million alert events with a
21.6\% noise rate from other detectors firing on the
same window --- an unscalable alert volume that no
operator can arbitrate by hand. Phase~1 (Dependable
Root-Cause Arbitration) reduces six single-signal
detectors to a five-class verdict through a
deterministic arbitration whose every cap, gate, and
tie-breaker is a principled consequence of an
observable cache signal. Phase~2 (Causal Direction
Anchoring) feeds the same cache to a Hybrid Causal
Attribution Framework anchored on Pearl's
Probability of Necessity and Sufficiency (\css),
with bootstrap CI and permutation $p$-value
providing a falsifiable significance claim on a
non-empty subset of events; the composite
scoring rule in \hcaf{} commits a calibrated
3-way scoring-rule output on the rest.

The empirical result on the 30-event v17 ground-truth is
$\frac{26}{30}$ (86.7\%) strict-correct
($\frac{26}{28} = 92.9\%$ excluding the negative control) on
Phase~1 --- a $+16.7$~percentage-point absolute gain over
the v18 pre-optimization baseline, achieved without
ground-truth-label leakage into the arbitration logic. The
four remaining misses are honest, documented, and not
algorithmic: they are detector-coverage limits, an upstream
API degradation, and a negative-control definition quirk
that the system's degraded-mode behavior surfaces explicitly.

We see three directions as the natural next step. First, a
held-out v20 evaluation on events unseen by any of the
arbitration thresholds, to confirm the fixes do not
overfit. Second, a Bayesian model-averaging extension of
the Phase-1 arbitration that replaces the hand-tuned
scoring-rule outputs over
[0, 1] candidate scores. Third, an
operational deployment of BGP Tracer as a continuous
Internet-anomaly attribution service, with the Phase-1
verdict driving operator alerting and the Phase-2
composite scoring-rule output driving post-incident
root-cause analysis.

We used an AI assistant (Cursor / Claude, model
versions documented in the released artifact) to assist
with the following parts of the paper:
(i)~initial drafting of the implementation scripts
in the \path{scripts/} directory, including the
detector ablations, threshold sensitivity sweeps,
leave-one-event-out analysis, and per-cause
detector fire-rate computation;
(ii)~reformatting of figures (matplotlib output to
publication-quality PDF via vectorised export); and
(iii)~light copy-editing of the abstract and
introduction to improve concision and parallel
structure.

All AI-generated content was subsequently reviewed,
verified, and revised by the authors. AI tools were
not used to generate empirical results, theoretical
claims, citations, or figures that the authors did
not subsequently verify. All quantitative numbers in
the paper (Tables~\ref{tab:method-comparison} --
\ref{tab:detector-ablation}, Figures~\ref{fig:motivation} --
\ref{fig:ablation}) come from the actual implementation
run on the v17 ground-truth; the authors verified every
number. Per DSN's policy, the AI assistant is not
listed as an author; all intellectual responsibility
remains with the human authors.
