\begin{abstract}
Internet operators increasingly rely on BGP monitoring platforms to detect routing anomalies, yet these platforms produce thousands of daily alerts while fewer than 1\% are ultimately confirmed by operators as actionable. Existing alert triage relies on static thresholds and heuristic rules, which cannot tell a routing anomaly that affects the business from one that does not. We present BGP Tracer, a two-phase alert-triage and attribution system that joins RIPE RIS BGP telemetry with Cloudflare Radar traffic aggregates on a common time grid. Phase 1 performs joint control-plane/data-plane correlation and returns a five-class verdict (\texttt{real\_incident} / \texttt{silent\_alert} / \texttt{traffic\_only} / \texttt{correlation\_uncertain} / \texttt{no\_clear\_anomaly}) together with the evidence behind it; each verdict is cache-replayable and the language model is confined to narrative rendering. Phase 2 is an optional deep dive that estimates a causal direction from a pre/post partition and abstains explicitly when the estimate is not statistically supported. On 29 curated ground-truth events, Phase 1 attains 89.7\% Top-1 cause classification. On a replay of 192 Cloudflare Radar alerts, 130 completed (62 harness failures) and BGP Tracer flagged 14 genuine incidents (10.8\% of completed replays, Wilson 95\% CI 6.5--17.3\%), a 9.3:1 reduction over the completed set at a median end-to-end latency of 225.6\,s. We also report a negative result: on the 25 real-cache events with a verified direction, the Phase-2 synthesis commits on 2 and is wrong on both, while single-channel baselines commit far more often. The evidence therefore supports joint routing--traffic arbitration with explicit uncertainty, not PNS-anchored direction attribution at current telemetry density.



\vspace{1ex}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} BGP routing, anomaly detection, causal inference, root-cause analysis, traffic attribution, network dependability.
\end{abstract}
