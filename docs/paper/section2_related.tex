\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

We organise related work in four buckets that mirror the
four question-types an Internet-anomaly attribution system
must answer, and map each bucket onto the dependability
property it covers. The cross-bucket quantitative
comparison is in \Tblref{tab:related}.

\paragraph{Routing anomaly detection (single-signal, no
arbitration).} ARTEMIS \cite{artemis} pioneered real-time
hijack detection using a push-based BGPStream listener and
a CAIDA AS-relationship graph for validation. BGPStream
\cite{bgpstream} is the de-facto data plane for academic
hijack research. The RIPE RIS \cite{ripe-ris} and
RouteViews \cite{routeviews} collectors expose the BGP
update streams these systems consume. Route-leak detection
has matured through the \msbd{} ``Peerlock'' mechanism
\cite{peerlock}, the NIST RPKI validator
\cite{rpki-validator}, and the MANRS guidance documents
\cite{manrs}. Each of these systems is \emph{single-signal}:
it classifies what happened on the routing plane but does
not adjudicate the data-plane consequence, and does not
arbitrate between leak vs hijack when both detectors fire
on the same window. From a dependability perspective,
single-signal detectors cannot fail-safe: a noisy detector
produces a noisy verdict.

\paragraph{Traffic anomaly detection (passive DNS / NetFlow /
Radar).} Cloudflare Radar \cite{cloudflare-radar} is the
canonical public source of Internet traffic aggregates; the
5-minute sampled time series it exposes is what BGP Tracer
consumes in Phase~1. ISP-internal NetFlow telemetry
\cite{kentik-blog, kentik} provides ground-truth labels for
several of the events. These systems are also
\emph{single-signal}: they flag a traffic anomaly but do
not adjudicate its cause and do not distinguish a real
outage from a real leak that happens to cause an
outage-like traffic drop.

\paragraph{Joint correlation and causal attribution.}
The closest prior work is the early literature on Granger
causality applied to BGP and traffic
\cite{granger1969,granger1980}, which suffers from the
non-stationarity objection raised by the v14 reviewer: BGP
update streams are bursty and non-stationary, and Granger's
stationarity pre-condition is systematically violated in
practice \cite{schottdorf14}.  Transfer-entropy-based approaches
\cite{transfer-entropy-internet} address non-linearity but
introduce a heavy hyper-parameter burden (binning strategy,
history length) and still produce a single statistical
verdict, not a five-class root-cause classification. The
most directly related prior work is the Hybrid Causal
Attribution Framework (\hcaf) published in an earlier
report \cite{hcaf-v1}. The prior \hcaf{} scored
\emph{causal direction} by \emph{weighted voting} across
four heuristic methods (MSBD, CTPR, CIC, Granger). A
weighted sum is functionally a vote with continuous weights
--- a perspective that has been criticised for failing to
(i)~resolve direction conflicts when the methods disagree,
(ii)~distinguish \emph{weak correlation} from \emph{weak
evidence}, and (iii)~produce a falsifiable significance
claim. The present work replaces the vote with a Pearl-PNS-\emph{motivated}
counterfactual \cite{pearl2009,spirtes00}. On the ground-truth the prior-vote
\hcaf{} commits a direction on $\leq 2$ events (the rest
abstain by tie-vote); our Pearl-anchored CSS+Bayesian
synthesis commits a calibrated scoring-rule output on a non-empty
subset and abstains honestly on the rest
(\Tblref{tab:related}).

\paragraph{Root-cause classification (closest to Phase~1
contribution).} The closest published work on root-cause
classification in BGP monitoring is the operational
post-mortem literature
\cite{cloudflare-blog, kentik-blog, apnic-blog} and the
MANRS audit format. These sources are post-hoc --- a human
reads the BGP update dumps, the Cloudflare Radar chart, and
the operator mailing-list report, then writes a verdict. A
separate strand of work addresses the \emph{dependability}
of anomaly attribution systems as a first-class concern.
The IETF's RFC~7908 \cite{rfc7908} and RFC~8212
\cite{rfc8212} codify ethics and BGP-route-leak prevention
by default; MANRS \cite{manrs} codifies operational best
practices. Each contributes a property a dependable system
must honour --- ethics, fault-prevention, operational
best-practice --- but none specifies an arbitration
algorithm for multi-detector conflicts. BGP Tracer's
Phase~1 arbitration (\Sectref{sec:arbitration}) is the
first published deterministic, no-LLM, multi-detector
arbitration that explicitly handles the three-way
(hijack, leak, outage) tie cases that dominate the
false-positive landscape of single-signal detectors.

\paragraph{Threat model and operational scope.}
BGP Tracer inherits the threat model of Cloudflare Radar
and RIPE RIS passive observers: the operator trusts the
data plane to be honest about its own reachability, and
the routing plane to be honest about its own
announcements. Adversarial injection of synthetic routing
or traffic signals is out of scope; this matches the
operational reality of any post-mortem analysis tool.
