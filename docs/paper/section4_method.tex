\section{Method: BGP Tracer}
\label{sec:method}

\noindent\emph{An end-to-end overview of the two-phase
pipeline (with degraded-mode paths and the shared
interpretability pivot) is in \figref{fig:workflow}
(Section~\ref{sec:intro}). This section gives the
detailed detector-by-detector and arbitration-by-arbitration
specification.}

BGP Tracer is a two-phase analyzer whose design priorities
are interpretability, reproducibility, and graceful
degradation. The two phases share a single cache (the
interpretability pivot); the Phase~1 verdict is the
user-facing root-cause classification; the Phase~2 verdict
is the explanatory causal-direction second layer.

\subsection{Phase 1: deterministic root-cause arbitration}
\label{sec:phase1}

The Phase~1 pipeline is the user-facing root-cause
classification layer that the the released evaluator evaluation scores. It
runs six deterministic detectors and a single arbitration
function. No LLM call is made in Phase~1. Phase~1 runs in
$\sim$60\,s per event on commodity hardware.

\paragraph{Detectors.}
The TrafficAgent consumes \cf{} Radar 5-minute aggregates
and emits an anomaly window (start, end, peak/trough drop
percentage). The RoutingAgent
consumes RIPE RIS / RouteViews \rrc{} BGP update dumps
and emits per-cause detectors: the Outage detector
counts BGP withdrawal events and produces
; the Route-Leak detector counts
candidate leak announcements, runs the post-filter
( path plausibility,
customer-vs-upstream direction), and emits a retained
event list plus a leak count
; the Origin/Forge-Hijack detector counts
hijack events and tracks self-recovery
re-announcements ; the \msbd{}
breakpoint detector finds the largest-mean-shift point
in the routing timeline (consumed by \css{} in Phase~2);
the \ctpr{} path reconstruction reconstructs the AS
path to the analysis AS (consumed by Phase~2); the
\cic{} cascade-influence counter counts the number of
ASes whose routing state is affected (consumed by
Phase~2). All six detectors are \emph{single-signal}: each
emits a structured payload with a count, a score, and a
list of retained events. The arbitration function
 is the
only component that combines them. The interpretability
property is the key: every Phase~1 verdict is traceable
to a detector output, and the arbitration is auditable
by reading .

\subsection{Phase-1 arbitration: \texttt{compute\_top\_cause}}
\label{sec:arbitration}

The arbitration function reduces the six detector outputs
to a single five-class verdict with a calibrated
confidence. The logic is a small, explicit decision tree
with four component layers, each of which is a direct
consequence of an observable cache signal:
\begin{enumerate}
\itemsep0pt
\item \textbf{Detector-derived candidate scores.} Each
detector's output is mapped to a candidate score in
$[0, 1]$.
\item \textbf{Caps} that prevent one detector from
dominating on weak evidence (e.g. capped
at $0.55$ when the outage detector fires weakly and the
leak detector fires strongly).
\item \textbf{Gates} that require a minimum evidence
threshold before a candidate can compete (e.g.
requires both the number of leak announcements exceeds 0 \emph{and} a non-empty
retained list).
\item \textbf{Tie-breakers} for cases where two
candidates land within $0.05$ of each other.
\end{enumerate}

The output is one of five classes:
, or
. The fifth class is the only
one that triggers a Phase~1 \texttt{unknown}; the other
four commit a label with a calibrated confidence in
$[0, 1]$.

\paragraph{Why a small, explicit decision tree, not a
learned classifier.}
A learned classifier trained on the ground-truth
would be the natural alternative, but it would fail the
no-data-leakage constraint: the model's decision
boundaries would be tuned to the ground-truth labels,
not to the cache signals. The explicit decision tree in
 has the counter-factual
property that every cap, gate, and tie-breaker is a
\emph{principled consequence} of an observable cache
signal (\Sectref{sec:fixes}) and the the released evaluator evaluation
scores it without ever reading the ground-truth labels.

\paragraph{Latency budget.}
Across 29 events, Phase~1 + arbitration total
$0.001$--$0.061$\,s (median $0.025$\,s); the LLM
narrative in Phase~2 contributes $58.7$\,s on a single
thread. The full pipeline stays below $90$\,s in the
production deployment, dominated by the LLM call rather
than the analytical core.

\subsection{Phase 2: CSS + Bayesian synthesis + LLM narrative}
\label{sec:phase2}

Phase~2 answers the second question BGP Tracer is
designed to answer: \emph{did the routing change cause
the traffic change?} The same cache is fed into the
CorrelationAnalyzer (computes the time offset $\Delta T$
between the routing and traffic intervention onsets),
the CausalAttributionEngine (runs the \css{} engine on
the two timelines, folds in 5 evidence channels under
a \emph{composite scoring rule} with a CSS-anchored
prior, and emits a calibrated 3-way scoring-rule
output over three
hypotheses: $R\!\to\!T$, $T\!\to\!R$,
\texttt{independent}), and the ReportAgent (renders a
structured HTML report with deterministic tables and
LLM-written prose; the LLM is forbidden from overriding
the CSS-anchored direction). The
scoring-rule framing reflects that the five evidence
channels share routing-side evidence and are not
strictly independent; we therefore call the
synthesis a composite scoring rule (\emph{not} a
generative-model Bayesian posterior) and rely on
the uniform-prior ablation to confirm the prior is
doing real work without over-riding the channels
(\Sectref{sec:uniform-prior}).

\subsection{5-channel weighted scoring rule (engineering decision)}
\label{sec:scoring-rule}

The CSS-anchored prior weight (a single number, not a full distribution) over the three causal
hypotheses
$\mathcal{H} = \{R\!\to\!T,\ T\!\to\!R,\ \text{independent}\}$:
\begin{equation}
P_0(h) \;\propto\; \begin{cases}
w_{\text{causal}} & \text{if } h = \mathrm{CSS.direction} \text{ and } \mathrm{CSS.is\_causal},\\
w_{\text{soft}} & \text{if } h = \mathrm{CSS.direction} \text{ and not } \mathrm{CSS.is\_causal},\\
1 & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:hcaf-prior}
\end{equation}
with $w_{\text{causal}}{=}10$ and $w_{\text{soft}}{=}4$.
The CSS-anchored prior prevents heuristic methods from
overriding a Pearl-anchored direction: when \css{}
returns a strict verdict ($p<0.05$ in
\Eqref{eq:css-perm}), the prior is sharpened by a factor
of 10.

Each of the 5 evidence channels contributes a
\emph{likelihood ratio} $\mathrm{LR}_k$ over the three
hypotheses, summarised in \Tblref{tab:lr-defs}. We
chose the five channels to span three orthogonal failure
modes: \emph{intervention detection} (\msbd{}),
\emph{path plausibility} (\ctpr{} + \cic{}), and
\emph{statistical baselines} (Granger, Transfer Entropy).
We \emph{excluded} Pearson lag-correlation: on it
abstains on most events because the \cf{} Radar 5-min
buckets are too sparse for Pearson's lag estimator to
converge; Transfer Entropy replaces it as the non-linear
directed-information channel.

The 5-channel weighted scoring rule combines the CSS-anchored prior weight
with the channel likelihoods as a discrete product of channel-specific contributions
(an engineering scoring rule, not a generative-model
Bayesian posterior):
\begin{equation}
S(h \mid \text{evidence}) \;\propto\; P_0(h) \cdot \prod_{k=1}^{5} \mathrm{LR}_k(h)^{\,\delta_k},
\label{eq:hcaf-score}
\end{equation}
where $\delta_k \in \{0.4, 1.0\}$ is the
\emph{disagreement discount} applied as an
\emph{exponent}: channels opposite the CSS-anchored
direction are down-weighted by $0.4$, agreeing channels
receive $1.0$. This implements Pearl's \emph{``no
information without corroboration''} principle:
heuristics refine, do not override, a Pearl-anchored
direction.

\paragraph{Abstention threshold.}
\hcaf{} declares \texttt{unknown} when the dominant
scoring-rule output mass falls below a one-third plurality
threshold:
\begin{equation}
\hat\tau \;=\; 0.34.
\label{eq:threshold}
\end{equation}
The choice is \emph{not} tuned on the ground-truth;
\Sectref{sec:sensitivity} sweeps
$\hat\tau \in [0.30, 0.60]$ and shows the the released evaluator Phase~2
strict-precision is insensitive to the exact value
within this range.

\paragraph{Channel independence and the uniform-prior ablation.}
\label{sec:uniform-prior}
The five channels are not strictly independent in a
generative-model sense (they share routing-side evidence);
we therefore do \emph{not} call the synthesis a
``Bayesian-style aggregation'' in the colloquial sense. We call it a
\emph{composite scoring rule} (\Eqref{eq:hcaf-score})
whose prior is the CSS-anchored counterfactual and whose
likelihood ratios are channel-specific. To bound the
prior's contribution,
runs the synthesis with the prior replaced by a uniform
prior over the three hypotheses. On the ground-truth
the uniform-prior run produces a flip rate of 6/30 = 20.0\%
on committed directions and an abstention rate of
$\leq 3/30$ (the same as the CSS-anchored run, because
both runs abstain on the structurally-sparse CSS events).
The 20.0\% flip rate is the share of events on which the
five channels disagree with the CSS-anchored direction by
enough to overturn the prior; on the remaining 80\% the
prior is consistent with the channel evidence, so the
prior is doing real work without over-riding the channels.
This 80/20 split is the headline result of the
uniform-prior ablation; it is what justifies calling the
CSS prior ``anchoring'' rather than ``overriding''.

\subsection{Degraded-mode behavior of the two-phase pipeline}
\label{sec:method-degradation}

The graceful-degradation design treats each input source
as a first-class degraded mode
(\Sectref{sec:reproducibility}). The two phases degrade
independently: Phase~1 always produces a verdict from the
available detectors (missing traffic data reduces the
verdict to \texttt{traffic\_anomaly\_only}, missing
routing data reduces it to the outage detector only);
Phase~2 gracefully reduces the 5-channel scoring-rule output to
the channels whose data is available. This is the
architectural reason the single no-cache miss in the the released evaluator
evaluation is explainable: the cache-rebuild failed, the
detector pipeline emitted \texttt{traffic\_anomaly\_only},
and the arbitration recorded the degradation in the
cache.
