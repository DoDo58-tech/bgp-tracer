\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

This section specifies what the system computes, in the order the pipeline
computes it: the shared time grid (\S\ref{sec:method-grid}), the Phase-1
detectors and their disambiguation rules (\S\ref{sec:method-phase1}), the
the paired displacement that Phase~2 measures (\S\ref{sec:method-est}), the
four conditions that make its answer admissible (\S\ref{sec:method-vetoes}),
and the vocabulary in which the procedure refuses
(\S\ref{sec:method-gate}). The separation that matters is between a
quantity and an authority: an effect size is informative inside the test
and still the wrong thing to order two edges by, so the direction is
decided by precedence and never by magnitude.

\subsection{The shared time grid}
\label{sec:method-grid}

Let $\mathcal{R}=\{(t_i,s_i^R)\}$ be the control-plane event log and
$\mathcal{T}=\{(u_j,s_j^T)\}$ the data-plane event log for one candidate
window $W$, each entry a detected anomaly with a timestamp and a severity
in $[0,1]$.

\begin{definition}[Shared grid]
\label{def:grid}
Let $t_0$ be the earliest timestamp in $W$ and let $\Delta=5$\,min.
Bucket $k$ covers $[t_0+k\Delta,\,t_0+(k+1)\Delta)$ and both series
are evaluated on the same index set $k=0,\dots,K-1$:
\[
x^R_k=\frac{1}{|E^R_k|}\sum_{(t,s)\in E^R_k} s,\qquad
x^T_k=\frac{1}{|E^T_k|}\sum_{(u,s)\in E^T_k} s,
\]
with $x_k=\varepsilon$ ($\varepsilon=10^{-9}$) when a bucket is empty
and $E^\bullet_k$ the events falling in bucket $k$.
\end{definition}

The two series are bucketed \emph{jointly}: $t_0$, $\Delta$ and $K$ come
from the union of the two logs, so index $k$ always denotes the same
wall-clock interval in both. Bucketing each log independently anchors the
grids at different epochs and makes index-wise comparison meaningless. An
empty bucket is a floor rather than a zero variance: a window in which
nothing was recorded is ``no observation'', and a dispersion estimate that
divides by it produces infinities that propagate into every downstream
statistic.

\subsection{Phase 1: detectors and arbitration}
\label{sec:method-phase1}

Phase~1 runs four detectors over the window. Each returns a structured
argument---fired or not, a confidence, the observations that produced it,
and a reason string when it stays silent---rather than a verdict. The
\emph{origin-hijack} detector compares the observed origin AS of each
announcement against the expected origin---a validated RPKI ROA where a
covering object exists~\cite{rpki-validator}, an inferred baseline
otherwise---and flags announcements whose origin contradicts it. The
\emph{route-leak} detector scores each announcement's AS path with a
path-probability model over a CAIDA AS-relationship snapshot~\cite{caida-asrel}:
the per-hop customer--provider and peer--peer likelihoods are composed
along the path, and a path whose composed likelihood falls below a fixed
threshold is reported as a leak candidate. A valley-free violation is one
way to drive that composed likelihood down, but the detector applies no
discrete valley-free test---it reports the composed probability, so the
threshold is a continuum rather than a yes/no structural predicate. It
reports a capped list, so
its count is a floor rather than an estimate, and the cap is recorded
alongside it. The \emph{outage} detector measures the withdrawal rate per
prefix against its own baseline and flags sustained withdrawal regimes. The
\emph{traffic} detector compares the observed five-minute series with a
baseline chosen by the data: a periodic daily/weekly profile when repeated
observations support one~\cite{cleveland-stl}, otherwise a robust
non-periodic estimate (median level plus MAD-based dispersion). It never
excludes an AS for being irregular; it changes the baseline instead (design
decision S2 in \S\ref{sec:intro}).

The detector outputs are combined by six disambiguation rules in a fixed
design-time order: leak-induced hijack, multi-actor leak, self-leak during
outage, pure-leak with a total traffic drop, ROA-versus-outage, and
leak-during-outage. The order is not tuned on the evaluation set
(Appendix~\ref{app:arbitration}). The arbitration maps the rule outcome
plus the detector states onto one of five verdict classes:
\texttt{real\_incident} (both signals anomalous and temporally related),
\texttt{silent\_alert} (routing anomaly, no traffic impact),
\texttt{traffic\_only} (traffic anomaly, no routing cause),
\texttt{correlation\_uncertain} (both present, alignment unestablished), and
\texttt{no\_clear\_anomaly} (neither). Two properties are deliberate: a
detector that did not run is recorded as \emph{suspended with a reason},
never as a negative observation; and the verdict records which rules fired,
so the mapping can be replayed from the cache.

\subsection{Phase 2: ordering the two edges}
\label{sec:method-est}

Phase~2 asks a narrower question than Phase~1: \emph{if} the two signals
are related, which edge moved first, and did the second move because of
it? No causal graph is available (\S\ref{sec:background}), so we do not
estimate the effect of routing on traffic. We test the conditions under
which a directional claim is admissible at all, and refuse when any of
them fails.

The estimator is \textsc{paddle}: \emph{Paired Displacement,
Difference-in-differences, with Ledger Evidence}. It is the only causal
estimator in the system, and the rest of this section specifies it.

\paragraph{The anchor is the data plane's trough.} The two sides are not
symmetric, and the estimator does not pretend that they are. A routing
change is sparse and bursty: a change-point search on the control plane
places its change point wherever the burst is, often hours away from the
incident, and the resulting lag becomes a property of the burst rather
than of the propagation. The traffic series is different: an impact is a
fall, and a fall has a well-located onset. So the trough $\tau_T$ of the
data plane supplies the anchor, and the control plane is measured
\emph{at} that moment.

\begin{definition}[Trough onset]
\label{def:trough}
Let $d_T(j)$ be the signed relative deviation of the $j$-th traffic
observation from its own baseline, so that $d_T(j)<0$ means a fall. The
\emph{trough onset} $\tau_T$ is the first observation of the
\emph{deepest} run of $r=2$ consecutive deviations below the floor
\[
-\max\bigl(1.5\,\mathrm{median}_j|d_T(j)|,\;0.02\bigr).
\]
\end{definition}

Two properties of this definition are load-bearing, and both were
defects before they were made. The onset is the first observation of
the run, not its deepest point: an impact holds the measured quantity
down for a while, and the deepest observation of such a plateau is
wherever the noise happened to reach its lowest---which can be hours
after the cause. Anchoring on it made the lag between the two edges a
property of the noise, and let two edges that moved \emph{together} be
ordered at all.

And the run that is chosen is the deepest one, not the first. A series
that wobbles crosses the floor often, and taking the first crossing
anchors the measurement on whichever excursion came first---which for
a real drop is usually not the drop. Choosing the deepest run keeps
both properties at once: the anchor is the onset of the event rather
than of the noise, and a transient wobble cannot displace a sustained
fall. On the planted mechanisms of \S\ref{sec:eval-direction}, making
this change moved the power of the estimator from $701$ to $2{,}342$
commitments out of $2{,}400$ windows.

\paragraph{The measurement is a paired difference-in-differences.} At the
anchor, the control plane's treatment arm is its rate in the slots around
$\tau_T$; the counterfactual arm is the same clock slots on the previous
days, for the same channel, measured by the same instrument. Both arms
are summarised by their \emph{median}, never their mean: BGP announcement
counts are heavy-tailed, and in an outage the one enormous slot is the
last burst before the silence, so a mean reads the outage as a
hundredfold surge and the congruence test then refuses an outage whose
whole signature is the opposite. The displacement is reported as a
multiple of the counterfactual, so one means normal, zero means silence,
and three means a threefold surge.

\begin{definition}[Paired displacement]
\label{def:displacement}
For channel $c$, let $O_c$ be the median of $c$'s rate in the
$\pm 3$ slots around $\tau_T$, and let $E_c$ be the median of the
same-clock counterfactual over those slots on the preceding days. The
\emph{paired displacement} is
\[
d_c=\frac{O_c}{E_c},\qquad |d_c-1|\ \text{the displacement from normal}.
\]
\end{definition}

\paragraph{The null is an ordinary day, at the same clock.} For each
anchor in the baseline period, the same measurement is applied to a day
known to be ordinary, giving a placebo distribution of $d_c$. The
comparison is a \emph{tail probability} rather than a ratio against a
quantile, because the two are not on the same scale: a channel's rate
cannot fall below zero, so the deepest silence an ordinary day reaches is
a ratio of exactly zero, while a surge has no ceiling. The placebos are
therefore split by direction and the incident is compared against the
distribution of its own kind,
\[
p=\frac{1+\#\{b:\ |d_b-1|\ge|d_c-1|,\ \mathrm{sign}(d_b-1)=\mathrm{sign}(d_c-1)\}}{1+\#\{b:\ \mathrm{sign}(d_b-1)=\mathrm{sign}(d_c-1)\}} .
\]
This is the test of \emph{exclusivity}: the control plane must deviate
more at the trough than it does at this hour on an ordinary day. The
number of placebos is reported with every verdict, because the smallest
attainable $p$ is $1/(B+1)$: a window that supplies six placebos can
reject at $\alpha=0.05$ only barely, and one that supplies none cannot
reject at all.

\paragraph{Silence is a measurement, and absence is not.} A channel whose
event-window series is flat at zero is normally read as "the control
plane did not move". For an outage that reading is backwards: the target
stops announcing its own routes, so the channel is silent \emph{precisely
when the incident happened}, and the same clock slots on the preceding
days carry a non-zero rate. A channel the instrument never carried is
zero everywhere, baseline included, and there is nothing to compare it
against; that one was not measured at all. The two are distinguished by
an explicit flag on the series, so that an outage can claim its silence
while an unmeasured channel cannot invent one.

\subsection{The four vetoes}
\label{sec:method-vetoes}
\label{sec:method-channels}

A claim is admitted only when four conditions hold
(Table~\ref{tab:vetoes}). They are tests, not scores: each has a null it
can fail, a failure mode, and a reason string that names the quantity that
failed. The conjunction is logical, so no weight, prior or floor binds
them, and a refusal points at the condition that produced it.

\centering
\caption{The four vetoes. A direction is committed only if every veto
passes; otherwise the first failure is returned as the reason, together
with the statistic that produced it. V3 suspends rather than fails when
the entity sets are unknown, so missing metadata cannot become a
refutation.}
\label{tab:vetoes}
\label{tab:channels}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.10\linewidth}p{0.20\linewidth}p{0.38\linewidth}p{0.24\linewidth}@{}}
\hline
\textbf{Veto} & \textbf{Null} & \textbf{Refuted when} & \textbf{Reason} \\
\hline
V1 precedence & the two edges are simultaneous & the control plane has
  no sustained deviation at or before $\tau_T$ within $5400$\,s &
  \texttt{inadmissible\_precedence}, \texttt{lag\_out\_of\_range} \\
V2 exclusivity & the response moves this much on its own & placebo
  $p\ge\alpha$ & \texttt{not\_exclusive} \\
V3 provenance & the observation cannot see the affected entity & the
  affected and the observed entity sets are disjoint &
  \texttt{provenance\_disjoint} \\
V4 congruence & the two movements disagree & the control plane's sign
  and the traffic's sign oppose each other &
  \texttt{mirror\_contradicted} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Precedence (V1).} The direction is read from the onset, never
from the size of the effect. The alternative---commit to the direction
with the larger contrast---is the natural heuristic and it is wrong for
the case that matters most: when a third event drives both series, the
later edge often carries the larger effect, so magnitude names the
response as the cause. A deviation counts only when it \emph{persists}:
the onset is the first slot of a run of three consecutive deviating
slots. A simultaneous shock then has its onset at the shock and the two
edges cannot be ordered.

\paragraph{Exclusivity (V2).} The placebo comparison above, together with
two absolute floors: the displacement must exceed $0.20$ in relative
units and $0.25$ as a multiple of the same-clock baseline rate. Without
the floors a channel that barely moves in both the incident and the
placebo could pass on a large ratio alone.

\paragraph{Provenance (V3).} A response can only be observed in a series
that covers the affected entities: if the routing edge concerns one prefix
set and the traffic aggregate is built over a disjoint set, a change in the
latter is not a response to the former. The check is a set intersection,
and it \emph{suspends} when either side is unknown. Unknown scope is
missing metadata, not a failed test, and the verdict records the
suspension so that it counts as neither a commit nor a refutation.

\paragraph{Conguence (V4).} The two movements must point the same way. A
routing \emph{surge} alongside a traffic \emph{collapse} is not the same
phenomenon, and reading the surge as the cause of the collapse would be
reading the consequence backwards. A routing silence alongside a traffic
collapse is the signature of an outage, and the two agree.

A fifth guard applies to $T\to R$ claims only. That direction asserts
that an operator reacted to observed load, so the routing change must be a
mechanism an operator can produce in response---an announcement or a path
change. A withdrawal is deliberately excluded: a withdrawal is what a
failure looks like in the control plane, and a failure and a traffic
drop share a common cause, so reading the withdrawal as a \emph{reaction}
to the drop would assert a direction the evidence does not support. An
origin change is a fault or a hijack and is never a reaction, and an
unannotated mechanism yields \texttt{mechanism\_unknown}. The asymmetry is
deliberate: an $R\to T$ claim needs no such guard, because traffic does
not choose to re-route.

\paragraph{What is deliberately absent.} There is no posterior over
hypotheses and no channel weighting. A posterior would need a prior over
causes and per-channel likelihoods, and two count series identify neither;
the resulting number would encode a preference over failure modes and
present it as a probability. Four tests whose size and power can be
measured separately (\S\ref{sec:eval-direction}) are preferable to one
number whose calibration cannot be checked. Nor are several statistics
scored and combined: the direction comes from one measurement, and the
estimator reports whether that measurement survives the four vetoes.

\subsection{Refusal semantics}
\label{sec:method-gate}

A refusal is a verdict. Every path returns a reason from a closed
vocabulary, and the artefact records the quantities that produced it: the
trough, the control plane's displacement, the placebo $p$ and its count,
the lag, the size of the shared scope, and the two signs. The vocabulary
exists so that a new code path cannot ship without a name an operator can
read, and so that abstention rates decompose by cause instead of collapsing
into one number that mixes them.

Two distinctions are load-bearing. First, \emph{structural} refusals
(\texttt{series\_too\_short}, \texttt{no\_response\_trough}) say that the
window cannot support the test, while \emph{substantive} refusals
(\texttt{not\_exclusive}, \texttt{provenance\_disjoint},
\texttt{mirror\_contradicted}, \texttt{inadmissible\_precedence}) say that
the test ran and the claim failed. Only the second group is evidence
against a causal link, and \S\ref{sec:eval-direction} reports them apart for
that reason. Second, suspension is not failure: an unknown scope is
recorded as such, so missing metadata can be neither laundered into a
refutation nor counted as a pass.

The ground-truth cause label is \emph{not} an input. Deriving a prior from
\texttt{event\_type} would be label leakage---the expected direction is a
function of that label---and \S\ref{sec:eval-protocol} describes the audit
that checks no detector reads it. A withdrawal observed inside the window
is a measured control-plane fact and enters only as the mechanism
annotation the $T\to R$ guard requires.
