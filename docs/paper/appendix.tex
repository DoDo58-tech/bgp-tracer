A.1 Coarse screen.  The candidate funnel begins with a fixed-threshold
screen. The quick score aggregates a mean-level shift and a dispersion
ratio against a week-over-week baseline, each capped and weighted, so
the score is monotone in the size of the deviation and independent of
absolute traffic volume. In the build evaluated here the admission
threshold is set so that every candidate reaching the screen is
evaluated downstream, which is why \S\ref{sec:eval-protocol} reports no
candidate-reduction claim: a reduction ratio computed from this stage
would characterise the threshold rather than the screen.
\label{app:regularity}

A.2 Arbitration rules.  Six disambiguation rules operate
on structured detector arguments in fixed design-time
order: \texttt{leak\_induced\_hijack}, \texttt{multi-actor
leak}, \texttt{self-leak during outage},
\texttt{pure-leak + total-traffic-drop},
\texttt{ROA-vs-outage}, \texttt{leak-during-outage}.
The ordering is not tuned on the evaluation set.
\label{app:arbitration}

A.3 Abstention analysis.  A refusal is a verdict, and every refusal
carries the veto that produced it together with the statistic behind it,
so abstentions are auditable rather than silent
(\S\ref{sec:method-vetoes}). The reasons form a closed vocabulary:
\texttt{inadmissible\_precedence} when the control plane has no
sustained deviation at or before the trough (V1);
\texttt{not\_exclusive} when an ordinary day at the same clock
reproduces the displacement as
well as the incident does (V2); \texttt{provenance\_disjoint} when the
two evidence sets do not share the affected prefix (V3, which suspends
rather than fails when the entity sets are unknown, so missing metadata
cannot become a refutation); and \texttt{mirror\_contradicted} when the
two movements point in opposite directions (V4). A
dependency that runs the wrong way in time returns
\texttt{reactive\_incompatible}. Each record holds the trough, the
control plane's displacement, the placebo $p$ and its count, the lag,
the size of the shared scope and the two signs, which is what
makes the coverage table of \S\ref{sec:eval-direction} interpretable one
window at a time.

A.4 Placebo resolution and the size of the test.\label{app:aea-size}
The V2 null is an empirical tail probability with the add-one correction,
so its smallest attainable $p$-value at $B$ matched placebos is $1/(B+1)$.
At $\alpha=0.05$ a window must therefore supply at least $19$ placebos of
the same direction before it can reject at all; the estimator requires
$20$. A window whose baseline cannot supply that many can never commit,
whatever its effect size, so the resolution of the placebo null is a
property of the window and not only of the rule.

The null is built from the same clock positions on the preceding days, so
a channel that wobbles on an ordinary day has already spent that wobble
against the null. This is why the size of the test is small rather than
nominal: over 400 windows per level the estimator commits $0.0\%$ of the
time at $\rho=0$, $0.0\%$ at $\rho=0.5$ and $0.0\%$ at $\rho=0.8$,
against a nominal $5\%$. We report the number rather than the nominal
level. A directional test that is very unlikely to commit without
evidence trades coverage for specificity, and the coverage this costs on
real windows is the subject of Table~\ref{tab:aea-coverage}.

A.5 Cache schema.  One JSON artefact per event, named by the AS of
interest and the event window, written atomically and carrying the
detector outputs, the arbitration record, the degraded-mode reasons and
the decision provenance.  Every verdict, detector argument, and
abstention record is reproducible from this single artefact.
\label{app:cache}

A.12b Which coordination stage earns its cost.\label{app:agent-ablation}
The degradation measurement asks whether the pipeline survives a failure.
The question this entry asks is different: of the six coordination
stages, which ones actually change the decision? We ablate each one in
turn through the engine's own stage switches---joint evidence generation,
PADDLE causal attribution, hypothesis generation, multi-round reasoning, the
Evidence Blackboard, and external enrichment---and re-run the identical
17-event corpus with the identical caches and scorer. Each arm disables
exactly one stage, so the delta against the full pipeline is attributable
to that stage alone. Because the pipeline calls a language model, a repeat
is an independent draw of its stochastic part, and the \emph{spread} column
of Table~\ref{tab:agent-component} is the run-to-run standard deviation of
the per-repeat accuracy, which is therefore the noise floor a delta has to
beat. Each arm was therefore run three times, and the delta is computed
\emph{paired within each repeat}---both arms see the same events in the
same repeat---so the pairing removes the event-level variance and the
bootstrap interval is over the three paired differences.

Table~\ref{tab:agent-component} is not a flattering table, and we present
it as measured. One stage moves the decision decisively: disabling
multi-round reasoning costs $0.118$ in accuracy, the paired difference is
negative in all three repeats, and its 95\,\% bootstrap interval
($[-0.118,-0.118]$) lies entirely below zero and far outside the noise
floor. The other five---joint evidence generation, PADDLE causal
attribution, hypothesis generation, the Evidence Blackboard, and external
enrichment---produce exactly the full pipeline's answers in all three
repeats, so we do not claim they are load-bearing: at $n=17$ a small
delta is not distinguishable from noise whatever sign it carries. No arm
scores \emph{higher} than the full pipeline; where a component cannot be
separated from the pipeline on this corpus, the pipeline's cost is not
buying a different label.

Scoring counts a resolved event whose pipeline run failed as a wrong
answer rather than dropping it, so an arm cannot improve its accuracy by
crashing on the events it cannot handle.

That the stages do not move the label is not thereby evidence that they
are useless: the Blackboard is what the auditability measurement in
\S\ref{sec:agent-explain} scores, and the reasoning and enrichment stages
are what the report and the trace are made of. The practical
recommendation is a staged one: a deployment that cares only about the
label can run the detector stack with arbitration and skip the rest, and
the expensive stages are justified by the artefact they produce rather
than by the accuracy they add.

A.13 Why the first version of the comparison was a wash.
\label{app:agent-wash}
An earlier version of this table read $19/39$ for both the agent and the
rules. The reason was measurable, and it was a defect in the arbitration
rather than in the agent: the cause axis let the agent's free-text proposal
override the deterministic physical reading whenever the agent's own stated
confidence cleared $0.60$. That gate is not a quality gate. On this corpus
the agent's confidence does not predict whether its override is right:
accuracy by confidence band is $0.50$, $0.40$ and $0.71$ for the
$0.7$--$0.8$, $0.8$--$0.9$ and $0.9$--$1.0$ bands, so the middle band is
\emph{less} accurate than the band below it. Gating on it let the agent
override the rule on $35$ of $39$ events, $18$ correct against $17$ wrong
---a coin flip (\texttt{results/eval/cause\_axis\_findings.json}).

What does separate the agent's correct overrides from its incorrect ones
is corroboration by an independent control-plane record. The hijack
detector's records carry the origin that announced a prefix; a record
whose origin is neither the victim nor one of the victim's legitimate
origins is a hijack claim, while a record that names the victim itself is
an artefact of a missing expected-origin baseline. Of the $16$ events on
which the agent and the rule originally disagreed, the agent's proposal was
corroborated on $10$ and it was right on $6$ of those; on the $6$
disagreements with no corroboration it was right on $0$. The arbitration
now asks for the record rather than for the agent's certainty, and adds one
further case: when the traffic layer produced no data at all, the physical
layer has no reachability measurement to offer and its answer is a
detector default, so the agent's contextual reading decides. Measured on
the corpus, this combination gained four events and lost none. The
methodological point is worth stating plainly because it is a trap: a
language model's confidence is not evidence about whether the model is
right.
