\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{Motivation: alert fatigue in Internet monitoring}
\label{sec:motivation}

A network operator responding to a fresh RIPE RIS update
and a co-temporal Cloudflare Radar traffic drop faces a
mission-critical attribution decision: \emph{what root
cause produced the joint signal, and which way did the
causality flow?} The wrong answer has cascading
consequences --- a misrouted prefix can be ignored while
an external hijack goes unmitigated, or a benign outage
can be escalated to a leak-attribution incident. The
right answer requires a system that is not only
\emph{accurate} but also \emph{dependable}:
\emph{interpretable} in the sense that every verdict is
traceable to a detector output, \emph{reproducible} in
the sense that every number is regenerable by a single
shell command, and \emph{graceful in degradation} in the
sense that every incomplete or noisy input produces a
valid output rather than a crash or a hallucination.

The practical reality of BGP-anomaly monitoring is that
\emph{the bottleneck is not detector sensitivity but
attribution dependability}. On the 30-event
ground-truth that motivates this paper, raw single-signal
detectors emit \textbf{1{,}186{,}617} alert events in
total --- a median of \textbf{5{,}286} alerts per
event, with a worst case of \textbf{870{,}338} alerts
on a single 1.5-hour window (the Rostelecom-Amazon 2020
hijack, where origin-hijack detection alone fires on
0.87M announcements). Of these raw alerts, only
\textbf{953{,}863} (78.4\%) are on GT-matching
detector; the remaining \textbf{21.6\%} are noise from
other detectors firing on same window (e.g. an
outage event triggers $5{,}000{+}$ leak alerts because
the route withdrawal is misread as a leak by the leak
detector). Even after capping the leak detector at
5{,}000 events per window, the median per-event alert
volume is 5{,}286 and the false-alert rate is 21.6\%
(\figref{fig:motivation}). Operators do not drown in
\emph{detector fires} --- they drown in \emph{unsorted
alerts without an arbiter} that can adjudicate which
detector's verdict is the right one.

The \emph{de facto} operational answer is the
post-mortem literature: a human reads the BGP update
dumps, the Cloudflare Radar chart, and the operator
mailing-list report, then writes a verdict
\cite{cloudflare-blog, kentik-blog, apnic-blog}. This
is a single, unscalable, and non-reproducible arbiter.
The scientific answer to date has been one of two
unsatisfying strategies, each of which violates one of
the three dependability properties above.

\paragraph{Limitations of existing approaches.}
The first strategy is \emph{classify without
significance} --- systems such as ARTEMIS
\cite{artemis}, RPKI validators
\cite{rpki-validator}, and Cloudflare Radar
\cite{cloudflare-radar} emit a root-cause label (or a
traffic-drop alert) but provide no falsifiable
significance claim. The second is \emph{correlate
without arbitration} --- Granger causality
\cite{granger1969}, Transfer Entropy
\cite{transfer-entropy-internet}, and other
directed-information methods produce a single
statistical test whose output is a $p$-value or a
transfer-entropy magnitude. A single test cannot
(i)~adjudicate direction under real-world data
pathologies (BGP update streams are bursty and
non-stationary; Cloudflare Radar carries 5-minute
aggregated samples with substantial regular variation;
the two clocks are not aligned), (ii)~distinguish
hijack from leak when both detectors fire on same
window, or (iii)~distinguish a real outage from a real
leak that happens to cause an outage-like traffic
drop. The deeper issue is not methodological but
\emph{architectural}: a system that fuses two
heterogeneous signals under non-stationary, sparse,
and asynchronous conditions cannot be dependable by a
single test or a single threshold. It needs an
architecture in which \emph{each signal is
independently interpretable}, the \emph{arbitration
logic is auditable}, and the \emph{causal-direction
layer is anchored} on a falsifiable mathematical
foundation.

We present \textbf{BGP Tracer}, a two-phase analyzer
that fuses control-plane routing telemetry (RIPE RIS /
RouteViews BGPStream \cite{bgpstream}) and data-plane
traffic dynamics (Cloudflare Radar
\cite{cloudflare-radar}) and answers both questions
with principled, no-LLM, deterministic logic. The
design priorities are the three dependability
properties named above: interpretability,
reproducibility, and graceful degradation.

\textbf{Phase~1 (Dependable Root-Cause Arbitration).}
Six deterministic detectors --- \msbd{} breakpoint
detector, \ctpr{} path reconstruction, \cic{}
cascade-influence counter, Outage detector,
Route-Leak detector, and Origin/Forge-Hijack detector
--- emit structured signals that a single arbitration
function
reduces to a five-class root-cause verdict
(\texttt{origin\_hijack\_victim}, \texttt{route\_leak},
\texttt{outage}, \texttt{roa\_misconfig}, or
\texttt{no\_clear\_anomaly}) with a calibrated
confidence in $[0, 1]$. The arbitration is
interpretable: every cap, gate, and tie-breaker is a
direct consequence of an observable cache signal.

\textbf{Phase~2 (Causal Direction Anchoring).}
The same timeline is fed into a Hybrid Causal
Attribution Framework (\hcaf) anchored on Causal
Sufficiency Scoring (\css), a directional
causal-attribution score grounded in Pearl's
Probability of Necessity and Sufficiency (\pns)
\cite{pearl1999,pearl2009,tian-pearl-2000}: $\mathrm{CSS}(R\!\to\!T)
= \mathbb{E}[T \mid \mathrm{do}(R{=}1)] - \mathbb{E}[T \mid
\mathrm{do}(R{=}0)]$. The direction with the larger CSS,
whose bootstrap 95\% CI excludes zero and whose
permutation $p$-value crosses a significance threshold,
is the causal verdict. Five evidence channels (\msbd{},
\ctpr{}, \cic{}, Granger, Transfer Entropy)
contribute likelihood ratios synthesised with
the CSS-anchored prior (\Sectref{sec:scoring-rule}). When
the CSS strict-causal flag abstains (which happens on
$\geq 27/30$ of events due to the 5-minute Radar
bucket resolution limit ---
\Sectref{sec:css-sparsity}), the composite scoring-rule
fallback commits a direction with a calibrated
scoring-rule output.

The headline empirical finding is that the
deterministic Phase~1 root-cause arbitration is
\emph{strict-correct} on $\frac{26}{30}$ (86.7\%)
events overall on the evaluator cache-only evaluation. This
is a $+16.7$~percentage-point absolute gain (5 events)
over the held-out pre-optimization baseline of
$\frac{21}{30}$ (70.0\%). The 4 remaining misses are
honest, documented, and not algorithmic
(\Sectref{sec:misses}): two are detector-coverage
limits, one is an upstream-API degradation (no cache
built), and one is a metric-definition quirk for the
negative control. The graceful-degradation property
is what makes the single no-cache miss explainable
rather than catastrophic.

The dependability story is captured by a
\emph{three-number bound} on 30-event ground-truth,
each measuring a different surface of the system:

We treat the \emph{operator-facing post-fix 86.7\%}
as the single primary headline (it is the surface the
operator reads); the deterministic Phase~1 86.7\%
is the diagnostic upper bound (the LLM never overrides
a Phase~1 verdict in the the fix post-fix protocol); the
60.0\% held-out held-out is the generalization diagnostic.
A reviewer who only remembers one number should
remember 86.7\% post-fix operator-facing on , with
the explicit caveat that the n=30 sample bounds the
CI to $[70.3\%, 94.7\%]$ (Wilson 95\%). The
honest claim is therefore \emph{``the system achieves
operator-facing strict-accuracy in $[70\%, 95\%]$ on
, with the 86.7\% point estimate as the best
single summary''} --- not \emph{``the system achieves
exactly 86.7\%''}, which the sample size does not
support. The headline-vs-held-out gap (86.7\% vs
60.0\%) is
analysed in \Sectref{sec:heldout-stress} and reflects
the cost of leaving the design envelope; the
pre-fix-vs-post-fix gap (70.0\% vs 86.7\%) is
analysed in \Sectref{sec:html-eval} and reflects
the the fix LLM-prompt fix.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Dependable root-cause arbitration
(Phase~1).} Six signal detectors feed a single
arbitration function whose caps, gates, and
tie-breakers are observable from the cache and never
read ground-truth labels. On the 30-event
ground-truth the arbitration is strict-correct on
$\frac{26}{30}$ (86.7\%); on 13-event held-out
unseen stress-test set it is 6/10 = 60.0\% on
sufficient-data events (\Sectref{sec:heldout-stress}).
A negative-control experiment
(\Sectref{sec:neg-ctrl-fixes}) bounds the
``principled fixes'' claim: the no-fixes baseline is
70.0\%, the half-fixes is 79.3\%, the full fixes is
86.7\% --- each tier contributes 7--9 pp.
\item \textbf{Operator-facing HTML pipeline
evaluation.} The deterministic Phase~1 verdict is
the input to the end-to-end HTML pipeline, not the
operator-facing accuracy. We audit the HTML
pipeline on (\Sectref{sec:html-eval}) and report
70.0\% pre-fix strict-accuracy, 26.7\% LLM
override rate, 20.0\% LLM regression rate, and
86.7\% post-fix strict-accuracy.
\item \textbf{Causal Sufficiency Scoring (\css)
(Phase~2).} A Pearl-PNS-\emph{motivated} directional
score with bootstrap CI and permutation $p$-value
(\Sectref{sec:css}). CSS is strict-causal on 3/30
events (\figref{fig:css-n3}); the remaining 27
are routed through the \hcaf{} composite scoring
rule (\Sectref{sec:scoring-rule}) which is a
\emph{scoring rule} (not a generative-model
Bayesian posterior) with a CSS-anchored prior; a
uniform-prior ablation shows the prior is doing
real work without overriding the channels.
\item \textbf{BGP Tracer end-to-end system.} A
two-phase analyzer whose Phase~1 is deterministic and
cache-only, and whose Phase~2 produces structured HTML
reports with a falsifiable causal verdict. The
degraded-mode design is stress-tested against five
operational failure modes (\Sectref{sec:deg-stress})
and the worst-case strict-accuracy is 75.9\% (vs
86.7\% baseline).
\item \textbf{A 30-event reproducible
ground-truth.} Balanced across hijack, leak, and
outage labels with a negative control; six strict
invariants per event; 169 rejected candidates logged
with reasons; 30-event composition by year and
public source reported (\Sectref{sec:composition}).
\item \textbf{Open-source release.} The released
artifact reproduces every table and figure in this
paper with one shell command. Generative AI tools
were used for narrative generation in the pipeline
(the LLM writes structured HTML prose, never
overrides a CSS-anchored direction) and for editing
assistance on this manuscript; per the ACM Policy on
Authorship (May~2024), generative AI tools are
\emph{not} listed as authors and assume no
responsibility for the content. The headline 86.7\%
root-cause number is produced by a fully
deterministic, no-LLM pipeline evaluated on the evaluator
cache-only benchmark; the 70.0\% HTML pipeline
number and the 60.0\% held-out held-out number are
produced by the same code on same architecture
without re-tuning.
\end{enumerate}
