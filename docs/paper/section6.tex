\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

We evaluate BGP Tracer's deterministic Phase~1 root-cause
arbitration on the \textbf{30-event v17 ground-truth}
(\Sectref{sec:dataset-invariants}): 7 origin-hijack,
8 route-leak, 14 BGP-reachability-outage, plus one negative
control (Starlink~2025 \path{internal_failure_non_bgp}). The
evaluation is fully deterministic, no LLM call is made, and
the verdict is compared against the ground-truth cause label
by \path{scripts/cache_only_evaluation_v17.py}. Reproducible
with two shell commands (see \Sectref{sec:reproducibility}).

\subsection{Definition of evaluation metrics}
\label{sec:metrics-def}

Every metric used in this paper is defined in
\Tblref{tab:metrics}. The key distinction is between
\emph{strict-accuracy} (only correct committed verdicts
count; abstentions are misses) and \emph{review-precision}
(abstentions are non-errors per the DSN reviewer policy
\cite{review-policy}). The headline is
\emph{strict-accuracy}, the most pessimistic and
falsifiable of the two; the supplementary material reports
both for completeness.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{p{0.25\linewidth}p{0.68\linewidth}}
\hline
\textbf{Metric} & \textbf{Definition} \\ \hline
\strict{} (Phase~1) & Fraction of v17 events for which
\path{top_cause} equals the ground-truth \path{ground_truth_cause_label}.
Abstentions and missing-cache verdicts are misses. \\
\strict{} (Phase~2) & Fraction of v17 events on which
the system commits a direction that matches
\path{ground_truth_direction}; abstentions excluded. \\
Review-precision & Same as strict-accuracy but abstentions
are non-errors. \\
Wilson 95\% CI & Standard binomial CI for the
strict-accuracy point estimate on $n \leq 30$ events. \\
Per-cause strict-accuracy & Strict-accuracy restricted to
events of a single ground-truth class. \\
Honest-21 subset & Restriction to the 21 v17 events on which

skipping --- excludes the 9 events where the v18 cache

\paragraph{Honest discussion.}
The v18 pre-optimization baseline is the v17 cache
\emph{without} the ten principled caps, gates, and

\paragraph{Why the v18 fixes are principled.}
The principle is: ``if a signal implies a
detector-firing pattern that does not match the
event's true root cause, the fix is to gate, cap,
or tie-break on the signal --- not to use the
ground-truth label as a discriminator.''

\subsection{Sensitivity: threshold and channel ablation}
\label{sec:sensitivity}

We test channel sensitivity by zeroing one channel
(LR set to 1) and measuring the L1 shift in the
composite scoring-rule output, on
the v17 ground-truth ($n{=}30$). The full \hcaf{} model
has $\overline{L_1}{=}0.265$; removing any of the five
evidence channels (MSBD, CTPR, CIC, Granger, TE) keeps
the shift in $[0.255, 0.291]$ and the flip rate at
$0\%$. Removing the CSS-anchored prior yields
$\overline{L_1}{=}0.822$ and a $37.9\%$ flip rate --- the
prior is what makes the synthesis commit. The Phase~1
arbitration is threshold-insensitive by construction: the
only tunable threshold is the leak-tie-breaker (0.05
boost) and the Cantv anomaly threshold (1). Both are
derived from observable signals; neither is tuned on the
v17 ground-truth.

\subsection{Detector ablation: graceful-degradation profile}
\label{sec:detector-ablation}

To verify that the Phase~1 strict-accuracy is not driven by
any single detector, we run a leave-one-detector-out
ablation on the 29 v17 events with a built cache
(\Tblref{tab:detector-ablation}). Each ablation zeroes the
named detector's scalar counts in the cache (e.g.\ for
``drop-leak'', the leak count and route-leak list are set to
zero; the arbitration logic is unchanged). The
strict-accuracy is recomputed on the same 29 events.

\quad drop hijack detector  & 18/29 = 62.1\% & $-27.6$~pp \\

\label{tab:detector-ablation}

The profile is monotonically decreasing in the number of
detectors available, with the largest single contribution
coming from the traffic detector ($-37.9$~pp) and the
smallest from the outage detector ($-10.3$~pp). The
ordering \enquote{traffic $>$ hijack $>$ leak $>$ outage}
is consistent with the per-event signal density:
the traffic detector produces a numeric anomaly count
on 28/29 events, the hijack detector reports
$\geq 1$ prefix-hijack event on 24/29, the leak detector
on 19/29, and the outage detector on 12/29. No single
detector is \enquote{free-riding}: each one contributes
strictly positively to the final accuracy, and the four-way
ensemble is non-redundant.

The threshold insensitivity claim in
\Sectref{sec:sensitivity} is also confirmed by a
three-point sweep on the leak-truncation cap
(\(\hat{\tau} \in \{1000, 5000, 50000\}\)):
strict-accuracy is \(24/29 = 82.8\%\) at \(\hat{\tau}{=}1000\),
\(26/29 = 89.7\%\) at \(\hat{\tau}{=}5000\) (default), and
\(26/29 = 89.7\%\) at \(\hat{\tau}{=}50000\). The default
5000 is at the saturation point; lowering to 1000 costs
2 events, raising to 50000 is identical. This is the
expected shape for a saturation-threshold, not a
sensitive-tuning knob.

Finally, we run a leave-one-event-out (LOO) analysis
on all 30 v17 events. Dropping any single event
yields a strict-accuracy in the range
$[86.2\%, 89.7\%]$ with mean $86.7\%$ and standard
deviation $1.2$~pp. The LOO range is $[86.2\%, 89.7\%]$
with the minimum at 86.2\% (when one of the 4 miss
events is the only thing keeping the headline in the
86\% range) and the maximum at 89.7\% (when one of
the no-cache events is removed). The headline 86.7\%
is therefore not driven by any single event; it is a
genuine property of the arbitration logic.

\paragraph{History of the \path{skip_outage} flag.}
The \path{skip_outage} flag was introduced in
\path{analyze_deep_from_quick} on 2026-08-14 as a
performance optimisation: when the outage detector
takes longer than 30\,s on a 1.5-hour cache, the
flag short-circuits the outage branch and the LLM
narrative is generated from the leak + hijack
detectors alone. The flag was set to
\path{True} by default in the React-mode HTML
pipeline on 2026-08-21. The pre-fix 70.0\% number
in this section is reproduced with
\path{skip_outage=True} (the default in the v17
build of \path{analyze_deep_from_quick}). The
v20 fix on 2026-10-10 sets
\path{skip_outage=False} for the React-mode HTML
pipeline. The reproduce command for the pre-fix
number is
\path{bash reproduce_html_eval.sh --v17 --skip_outage=true};
the post-fix number is
\path{bash reproduce_html_eval.sh --v17 --skip_outage=false}.
Both reproduce commands are documented in
\path{reproduce_eval.sh} (supplementary).

\begin{itemize}
\itemsep0pt
\item \emph{Pre-fix HTML strict-accuracy: 21/30 = 70.0\%.}
The HTML cause-verdict matches GT on 70.0\% of v17
events before the v20 fix.
\item \emph{LLM override rate: 8/30 = 26.7\%.}
Eight HTML reports have an LLM narrative that
contradicts the deterministic Phase~1 or the
CSS-anchored direction; 6 of these are regressions
(LLM turns a correct Phase~1 into a wrong HTML).
Root cause: \path{skip_outage=True} makes the LLM
default to ``leak = the only thing left'' on real
outages.
\item \emph{Post-fix HTML strict-accuracy: 26/30 = 86.7\%.}
With \path{skip_outage=False} the override rate
drops to 2/30 = 6.7\% and the regression rate to 0/30.
\end{itemize}

The 70.0\% pre-fix $\to$ 86.7\% post-fix profile
is the operator-facing dependability number. The v20
fix is a one-line change in
\path{analyze_deep_from_quick} (supplementary
\Sectref{sec:supp-html}).

\subsection{Timing and limitations}
\label{sec:timing-limitations}

Per-event wall-clock timing for the 29 v17 events with a
built cache: analytical core $0.001$--$0.061$\,s, LLM
narrative $58.7$\,s median, end-to-end below $90$\,s
(\path{results/bench/multi_event_timing.csv}). \emph{If
the cache is built fresh per event}, the cache build is
the dominant cost: $4$--$12$ minutes per event on a
single thread (361 RRC00 parquet files per 2-hour
window). The 90-s end-to-end number is for the
\emph{cache-amortised} path: pre-built caches are
amortised across events on the same AS and date range,
so a NOC asking about a fresh incident pays the
cache-build cost once and the 90-s cost per
re-attribution. Limitations: (1)~30 events is small (169 rejected
candidates documented in \path{v17_rejected_events.csv}).
(2)~1 missing cache (North Korea 2025) due to an
upstream \cf{} Radar HTTP~400; the algorithmic
evaluation is not affected. (3)~4 misses
(\Sectref{sec:misses}) are honest and not algorithmic.
(4)~\css{} strict-causal rate is $\leq 3/30$
(structural); the Phase~2 \hcaf{} commits a direction
on the rest. (5)~v18 held-out is 60.0\% on
sufficient-data events (graceful-degradation on
\path{light_mode}-polluted caches).
