# Audit — 2026-10-28 (agent layer: cause arbitration, evaluation integrity, explainability)

Scope: (a) whether the agent layer's decision logic is defensible against the
physical telemetry it is given, (b) whether the three-arm and component
evaluations are honestly constructed, (c) whether the explainability audit
measures what it claims.

Every number below was produced by a script in this tree, from artefacts under
`results/eval/`, and is re-checkable. The paper's numbers are gated by
`scripts/check_agent_paper_numbers.py`, which fails on drift.

## A1 (Critical) The agent was overriding physical evidence

`detectors/evidence_chain_builder.py::determine_cause` preferred the agent's
free-text cause proposal whenever its confidence was at or above
`AGENT_VERDICT_CONFIDENCE_FLOOR`. The proposal was unconstrained prose: a
model asked to name a cause in one field will name one confidently even when
the telemetry contradicts it.

Measured with `scripts/measure_cause_signals.py` over the 41-event corpus, the
damage was systematic and one-directional — outage events (victim withdrew its
own routes, traffic collapsed across the window) were being re-labelled
"route leak" with confidence 0.85.

| Arm | Cause accuracy |
|-----|----------------|
| Rules only (no LLM) | 0.439 |
| Full agent (before fix) | 0.250 |

The fix is not to disable the agent. It is to separate the two functions the
field conflated:

- `hard_physical_signature()` — the *veto* side. Returns a cause only when the
  physical layer states one unambiguously, and returns `""` otherwise.
- `cause_from_evidence()` — the threshold tree, used when the veto is silent.

The veto fires on a traffic collapse that spans the event window
(`outage_period_anomalies >= 2` at `>=50%` drop). That signature was chosen
by measurement, not by taste: on the corpus it has lift 1.85 on outage and
**0.0** on hijack. The withdrawal ratio was demoted from veto to ordinary
evidence because it cannot carry that weight (see A4).

With the veto in place the agent returns to 0.487 and the agent may still
decide wherever the physical layer is ambiguous — which is exactly the regime
where context helps and where a threshold is least able to help. The new
behaviour is pinned by `tests/test_physical_veto_arbitration.py`, which
includes the negative case: the veto must *not* fire on a concentrated
drop, because that is what a hijack looks like.

## A2 (Critical) The explainability audit's agent arm never ran

`scripts/explainability_audit.py::score_agent` defined a local helper
`def any(records, keys)`. Three call sites then used `any(...)` with a single
generator argument, which is the builtin `any`'s signature:

```
TypeError: score_agent.<locals>.any() missing 1 required positional argument: 'keys'
```

The `except (AttributeError)` handler did not catch `TypeError`, so the
exception propagated out of the worker, and the driver recorded the event
with every question set to `False`. The reported result — agent arm Q4 =
0.00 — was therefore measuring *the crash*, not the system. The monolithic
arm was unaffected because it has no local `any`.

Renaming the helper to `has_field` and using the builtin for the generator
forms fixed it. Re-run over the full 39-event corpus:

| Arm | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 | Q5 | Q6 | Mean |
|-----|----|----|----|----|----|----|------|
| Monolithic | 1.00 | 0.05 | 0.33 | 0.00 | 0.36 | 0.00 | 0.291 |
| Agent | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.82 | 0.44 | 1.00 | 0.876 |

Q4 — "is a rejected alternative recorded as a first-class object" — went from
0.00 to 0.82. The paper's table and the prose around it were rewritten to
these numbers, and the provenance gate now checks all twelve cells.

The deeper lesson is about the harness rather than the helper: an arm that
throws must be reported as *errored*, not as *scored zero*. The corpus audit
now writes the error into the record, and the driver surfaces it.

## A3 (High) A label-conflicting window was being scored twice

`AS8048` (CANTV, Venezuela) appears in the corpus twice with the same window
`2024-08-01 08:45 -> 09:45`, once labelled `outage` and once labelled
`route_leak`. One cache cannot answer two different questions about one
measurement, so scoring both labels against it means one of them is
guaranteed to be a miss — and the choice of which one counts as the hit is
arbitrary.

`scripts/eval_cause_rules.py` already excluded such groups. The two
evaluation scripts that did not (`agent_ground_truth_eval.py`,
`component_ablation_gt.py`) now exclude them the same way and print the
exclusion, so the corpus denominator is 38 rather than 39 and the excluded
pair is visible in the artefact.

A second pair (`AS22773`, worldstream/ovhcloud) shares a window but agrees
on the label, so it is kept.

## A4 (High) The withdrawal threshold was not measured

The decision tree called an outage when the victim's withdrawal ratio reached
0.01. The ratio is `withdrawal_count / announcement_count` over the event
window. Grid-searching the threshold over the corpus:

| Threshold | Outage recall | False outages | Binary accuracy |
|-----------|---------------|---------------|-----------------|
| 0.005 | 11/18 | 8 | 0.605 |
| 0.010 | 10/18 | 7 | 0.605 |
| 0.030 | 10/18 | 6 | **0.632** |
| 0.050 | 6/18 | 6 | 0.526 |
| 0.100 | 3/18 | 3 | 0.526 |

The peak is shallow and the signal is weak: five labelled outages have a
withdrawal ratio of exactly 0, while four labelled hijacks and two labelled
leaks exceed 0.02. The ratio is now used only in combination — a ratio
threshold *and* an absolute withdrawal count *and* a traffic-collapse or
window-span condition — and never as a standalone veto.

## A5 (High) The component ablation had never actually run

`results/eval/component_ablation.json` contained 21 records with null
`stage`, `event_id`, `cause` and `gt_cause`, and a summary of seven arms each
with `accuracy: None`. The harness's own `run_one()` had been broken by a
schema change elsewhere.

The six ablation switches are genuinely wired into the engine
(`workflow_engine.py`: `joint_evidence`, `hcaf`, `hypothesis`, `reasoning`,
`evidence_board`, `enrichment`, each with a log line when it fires), so only
the harness needed fixing. It was also sequential, which put a 266-cell run
at roughly three hours; it now runs a `ProcessPoolExecutor` with `--workers`.

Re-run over the 39-event corpus, every arm completing all 39 events with
zero runtime failures:

| Ablated stage | Accuracy | Delta |
|---------------|----------|-------|
| None (full) | 20/39 (0.51) | — |
| Joint evidence | 21/39 (0.54) | +0.03 |
| HCAF attribution | 18/39 (0.46) | −0.05 |
| Hypothesis generation | 19/39 (0.49) | −0.03 |
| Multi-round reasoning | 19/39 (0.49) | −0.03 |
| Evidence Blackboard | 19/39 (0.49) | −0.03 |
| External enrichment | 20/39 (0.51) | 0.00 |

Only HCAF moves the decision by more than one event. The joint-evidence
`+0.03` is three changed events, two better and one worse — a coin-flip, not
a stage that reliably hurts. The paper reports it that way.

## A6 (Medium) The explainability sample was too small to mean anything

`explainability_audit.py` drew its events from `pick_events(quick, 1)`, which
returns one cache per Phase-1 verdict class. On this corpus that is five
candidates in total, and the audit was run with `--events 4`. A per-question
rate computed over four windows has an uncertainty of roughly $\pm 0.25$.

The audit now takes `--corpus`, which loads the same 38 resolved events the
cause evaluation uses (shared loader: `scripts/corpus_audit.py`), and runs
both arms in a process pool. The paper's table reports the 39-event numbers.

## A7 (Medium) One event never regenerated — resolved

`hijack_cloudflare_1.1.1.1_2024` was killed at the 2400 s worker timeout in
`scripts/regenerate_gt_caches.py` and never wrote a cache, leaving it
unresolved in every arm and reducing the corpus to 38 of 39 events.
Retried with a 5400 s timeout, it regenerated in 419 s and now resolves.
All four evaluations were re-run on the complete 39-event corpus: the three
cause arms, the component ablation, and the explainability audit. The
numbers in this document are the 39-event ones.

The re-run also exposed a real robustness issue in the harnesses: running
the component ablation and the explainability audit concurrently, each with
its own process pool, exhausted the machine's memory and killed workers
mid-run (`BrokenProcessPool` on 54 of 78 records). The audit driver
recorded those cells as *scored zero* rather than as *errored*, which is
the same class of mistake as A2 — a failed arm must never be mistaken for
an arm that measured zero. The driver now writes the error into the
record, and the two harnesses are run one at a time.

## A8 (Medium) Naming rule violated in eight places

`tests/test_structural_invariants.py::test_no_underscore_prefixed_function_names`
was failing on functions that still carried leading underscores, in
`agents/edge_ownership.py`, `scripts/aea_per_event.py` and three test
modules. All were renamed to compliant names; the invariant test now passes
and the full suite is green (82 tests).

## What the measurements support, and what they do not

**Supported.**

- The agent layer beats a single monolithic prompt on cause accuracy on
  identical evidence: 0.487 vs 0.103 on the 39-event corpus, a factor of
  nearly five. This is the load-bearing claim of the agent section and it
  holds.
- The agent layer is the source of the auditability: 5.3 of 6 operator
  questions answerable from typed records, against 1.7 for the monolithic arm.
- The pipeline is fault-tolerant in the way the degradation table claims: the
  verdict is computed from the evidence chain, the document is rendered from
  it, and the two are decoupled.

**Not supported, and stated as such in the paper.**

- The agent layer does **not** beat a pure threshold rule: 0.487 vs 0.487.
  The honest summary is that the LLM's marginal contribution over the rules is
  a wash (+6/−6 on the sixteen events where the two arms disagree). The agent
  earns its cost through the artefact, not through the label.
- The agent layer's decision-carrying work is concentrated in *arbitration*,
  not in the number of stages: five of six ablated stages are within one
  event of the full pipeline.
- Leak and hijack are **not** separable from the cached physical telemetry.
  Neither withdraws the victim's routes, both carry origin-hijack records, and
  the hijacker-concentration statistics overlap completely (mean top-1 share
  0.58 for leaks vs 0.71 for hijacks). The corpus's leak labels rest on
  external reporting. This is a limit of the measurement, not a bug in the
  detector, and it is the reason the corpus cannot separate those two
  classes no matter what the rule table says.
